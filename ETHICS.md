# Ethics

> Project author: Petr Kosov <p.b.kosov@yandex.ru>

This document expresses project commitments. It does not add conditions
to the Apache License 2.0.

## Epistemic honesty

Math-coding must not present structural validity as proof of software
correctness. Unknown, missing, stale, waived, reviewed, and observed
are different states and must remain distinguishable.

## Proportionality

Process cost should be proportional to potential harm, uncertainty,
and irreversibility. Routine safe work should remain quiet. Critical
changes should receive stronger evidence and independent scrutiny.

## Human authority

An inference made by an agent is not a policy decision. Agents may
find risks and propose obligations, but acceptance of consequential
tradeoffs belongs to an accountable authority.

## Explainable intervention

A blocking decision must identify the applicable rule, affected
subject, reason, and practical remedies. Emergency work must have a
faster, explicit break-glass path rather than an incentive to bypass
the system.

## Privacy and restraint

Do not store private chain-of-thought, secrets, unnecessary personal
data, or complete conversation transcripts. Retain only the smallest
reviewed knowledge needed to preserve engineering context.

## Non-surveillance

Process metrics exist to improve the method, not to rank individuals.
Do not use token counts, waiver counts, review disagreement, or defect
history as employee performance scores.

## Portability

The protocol should work locally, offline, and without a required
service. It should accept established industry formats instead of
replacing them and avoid dependence on any one model, forge, vendor, or
programming language.

## Correctability

Errors may be corrected without erasing their history or stigmatizing
the author. Acknowledging uncertainty and revising a decision are
signs of a working assurance process.

## Self-application

The project must publish known limitations, measure its own costs,
and use the currently active rules when changing those rules.
