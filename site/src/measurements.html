Measurements

A method is judged by the result it produces, not by the number of claims it accumulates. This page separates what has been observed from what is still only a criterion.

Unit, integration and package checks do not prove a gain in development speed. Until the three-arm benchmark is executed and reviewed independently, the honest statement is: the method is verified to work, and its benefit is still an open question.

How a measurement is decided

  1. The task set is declared before the run. Tasks, acceptance criteria and the order of independent review are fixed in advance.
  2. All outcomes count. Unsuccessful runs are recorded, not discarded.
  3. The numbers are about the result. Time to an accepted result, handoff and repeated research, defects found after acceptance, returns to rework, false “everything is verified” claims, manual actions, and the real time, tokens and cost.
  4. The comparison decides, not the count. Two independent losses against the plain checklist mean the mechanism is simplified or removed — not extended.

A green map says the declared claims hold. It says nothing about whether the method is worth its cost — that question has its own experiment, and it is still open.
