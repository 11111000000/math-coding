The three-arm comparison of 2026-11-22

The comparison was run on 2026-11-22. It did not confirm the hypothesis of the project, and the numbers are given here as they stand.

Three real tasks in three projects: a change with a handoff in yeah, a weak existing verification in Genium-clean, a decision without an ADR in agents-writing-skills. Three arms — plain work, a short checklist, the full protocol — one repeat each, one model. The observer was blind to the arm, but came from the same culture.

Nine runs in all, every one of them written into the ledger. The protocol arm also paid 5001 s of the machine time of the method’s own gate — 83 minutes on top of the work of the executor.

The hypothesis did not hold. The checklist was no worse than the protocol on acceptance, on cost or on the defects found after acceptance, and the protocol paid 83 minutes of machine gate on top. The plain arm was accepted twice of three, so it had fewer accepted results in which a defect could still be found. What did show up is the qualitative advantage of the method: a negative control catches a broken verifier that an exit code cannot.

The observation window of seven days is still open, so this is a direction and not an effect; the sample is three tasks and one model. The honest statement is therefore: the method is verified to work mechanically, its benefit is still unproven, and the first comparison points at the checklist. The protocol of the comparison lives in this repository: docs/measurements/BENCHMARK-PROTOCOL.md.

How a measurement is decided

  1. The task set is declared before the run. Tasks, acceptance criteria and the order of independent review are fixed in advance.
  2. All outcomes count. Unsuccessful runs are recorded, not discarded.
  3. The numbers are about the result. Time to an accepted result, handoff and repeated research, defects found after acceptance, returns to rework, false “everything is verified” claims, manual actions, and the real time, tokens and cost.
  4. The comparison decides, not the count. Two independent losses against the plain checklist mean the mechanism is simplified or removed — not extended.

A green map says the declared claims hold. It says nothing about whether the method is worth its cost — the first comparison of the method did not confirm the hypothesis, and its window is still open.
